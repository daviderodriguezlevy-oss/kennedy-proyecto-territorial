# Five Mechanisms Turn Twelve Interactions Into Agents

Every interaction on the simulator's list can be written as an agent rule backed by published evidence, and the twelve interactions need only five underlying mechanisms. The first is a **consumer contact** whose lethality, number of victims and duration are parameters; it covers predation, herbivory, parasitism, parasitoidism and hematophagy. The second is a **contest** with a probabilistic winner and a steering impulse for winner and loser; it covers aggression, interference, kleptoparasitism, territoriality and mobbing. The third is **cargo** that rides on an agent until a timer or hazard drops it; it covers seed dispersal, phoresy, epibiosis and attached ticks. The fourth is **occupancy** of a substrate that the agent seeks only in the right reproductive state; it covers nesting, oviposition and brood parasitism. The fifth is a **diffusing, decaying field**; it covers positive and negative allelopathy, scent-marked territories, pheromone trails and odour plumes. Symbiosis, mutualism and floral visitation reuse the consumer contact with a benefit function attached. The formal vocabulary is less complete than the behaviour literature. The OBO Relation Ontology (RO, release 2026-09-04) and GloBI's source code give terms for predation, parasitism and its subtypes, pollination, floral visitation, allelopathy, epiphytism and egg-laying. **RO has no relation for herbivory, hematophagy, aggression, competition, nesting, animal-mediated dispersal or animal-on-animal epibiosis.** GloBI fills some of these with labels that carry no RO identifier, and allelopathy's sign must be stored as an attribute because RO does not split positive from negative. On movement, the herd and insect literature now supports a merged table of 33 movement classes across air, land and water. The richest is the sheep herd: three states (graze, walk, run), switches whose rates rise with the number of neighbours already switched, metric alignment at about 1 m and Voronoi interactions when running ([Ginelli et al. 2015](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/files/58184052/PNAS_2015_Ginelli_12729_34.pdf)). Herds, ants, marching locusts, whirligig beetles and mosquito swarms differ mainly in neighbourhood type, whether agents align, and their state machine. The main remaining weaknesses are physical constants: no sourced diffusion or release rates for allelochemicals, no measured drag cost of epibionts, and no movement-level model of tsetse, leech or vampire-bat foraging. These must be treated as calibration parameters, not facts.

## Twelve interactions reduce to five agent mechanisms

The central synthesis of this round is that the user's interaction list does not need twelve separate code paths. Lafferty & Kuris classified consumer strategies with four yes/no questions: one victim or several per life stage, whether the victim's fitness is eliminated, whether host death is required, and whether harm grows with parasite load ([Lafferty & Kuris 2002](https://parasitology.msi.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/docs/publications/trophic%20strats.pdf)). Under that scheme predators, micropredators (mosquitoes, leeches, lampreys), typical parasites, parasitoids and parasitic castrators become parameter settings of a single contact behaviour. Aggression in all its forms reduces to an encounter test, a probabilistic winner and a pair of steering impulses, as DomWorld shows ([Hemelrijk 1999](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1999.0646)). Mechanistic seed-dispersal models all use the same recipe: ingest, draw a retention time, move the animal with its own rules, deposit ([Jones et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3113)). That recipe also covers burrs, phoronts, epibionts and ticks. Nest and oviposition choice share a steer → accept → occupy → recruit sequence. Allelopathy, territorial scent and ant pheromone are all a scalar field with emission, decay and diffusion. The table below is the researchers' synthesis. The anchors are the sources that supply each rule's structure.

Kéfi and colleagues gave the population-level counterpart of this table. They sorted non-trophic interactions into three functional classes according to which parameter of a dynamic food-web model they modify ([Kéfi et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2011.01732.x)). Miele et al. implemented six such types in a bioenergetic model. Competition for space and recruitment facilitation modify growth. Predator interference (a Beddington–DeAngelis term) and refuge provision modify feeding. Increased and decreased mortality modify mortality. Their intensity ranges were roughly c₀ = 0.0045–0.012, i₀ = 0.75–3.0, r₀ = 0.25–1.75, e₀ = 0.15–1.8, n₀ = 0.75–3.0 and p₀ = 5–20 ([Miele et al. 2019](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1007269); values [P], read through a summary). For an agent simulator the mapping is direct. A **feeding-rate modifier** becomes a steering or encounter modifier, a **mortality modifier** becomes a survival hazard, and a **growth/recruitment modifier** becomes a change in reproduction or establishment probability. The stakes are not small. Across 58 food webs in seven ecosystems, webs that contained foundation species had 2.1× more species and 1.6× higher link density ([van der Zee et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0199152)). In three Californian estuaries, parasite biomass exceeded that of top predators ([Kuris et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature06970)). A simulator that omits these layers misses a large share of both structure and energy flow.

### Five details in the vocabulary change how the network should be stored

**First, allelopathy's sign lives outside the ontology.** RO_0002555 "allelopath of" is defined as a relationship "where one organism is influenced by the biochemicals produced by another". Its comment says allelopathy releases chemicals "to positively or negatively influence the growth, survival or reproduction of other organisms" ([ro.obo](https://github.com/oborel/obo-relations/blob/master/ro.obo)). Positive and negative allelopathy are therefore one RO edge with a sign or dose-response attribute. The definition's wording also reads in the inverse direction from its own `seeAlso` gloss ("x is an allelopath of y if x produces…"). The gloss is the safer guide: the actor is the emitter.

**Second, several GloBI terms imply others.** In GloBI's internal hierarchy, `preysOn` implies `eats` and `kills`. `pollinates` implies `visitsFlowersOf`, `hasHost` and `symbiontOf`. `dispersalVectorOf` implies `hostOf`, `symbiontOf` and `vectorOf`. `ectoparasiteOf` implies `parasiteOf`, `livesOn`, `eats` and `damages` ([InteractTypeUtil.java](https://github.com/globalbioticinteractions/globalbioticinteractions/blob/main/eol-globi-lib/src/main/java/org/eol/globi/domain/InteractTypeUtil.java)). When the simulator imports GloBI records, a `pollinates` record should therefore also enable the floral-visitation encounter. A `dispersalVectorOf` record should not be read as a pathogen vector.

**Third, dispersal and pathogen vectors collide on import.** GloBI maps EOL's `DispersalVector` and `HasDispersalVector` onto RO `vectorOf` (RO_0002459) and `hasVector` (RO_0002460), the same identifiers used for disease vectors ([InteractType.java](https://github.com/globalbioticinteractions/globalbioticinteractions/blob/main/eol-globi-lib/src/main/java/org/eol/globi/domain/InteractType.java)). An importer must disambiguate by target kingdom: a plant target means seed dispersal, a pathogen target means disease transmission.

**Fourth, seven RO interaction relations have no textual definition.** These are `kills`, `visits`, `visits flowers of`, `pollinates`, `lays eggs in`, `host of` and `is vector for`. Any schema "definition" column must quote GloBI usage or write its own wording ([ro.obo](https://github.com/oborel/obo-relations/blob/master/ro.obo)).

**Fifth, coarse sign classes exist in other vocabularies.** Mangal accepts twelve flat type values: competition, amensalism, neutralism, commensalism, mutualism, parasitism, predation, herbivory, symbiosis, scavenger, detritivore and unspecified ([rmangal](https://github.com/ropensci/rmangal/blob/master/R/search_interactions.R)). That list is a convenient sign class to store beside the GloBI label, and it is the only place where "herbivory" and "competition" appear as controlled terms. GloBI's kleptoparasitism pair (RO_0008503/0008504) and roosting terms (`coRoostsWith` RO_0002801, `hasRoost` RO_0008509) cover two behaviours the user's list implies but does not name. The practical encoding that follows is to store, for every edge, a GloBI label (always present), an RO IRI (nullable), the nearest broader RO term, a sign pair, and the Kéfi functional class.

## Blood-feeders and parasites are consumers with a contact-duration dial

### Lafferty and Kuris place hematophagy between predation and parasitism

Lafferty & Kuris's key resolves the ambiguity in the user's list. A **micropredator** is "a predator that does not kill its prey (by eating only part of it; e.g. mosquito)". It attacks several victims per life stage, and their examples are mosquitoes, leeches, lampreys and cookie-cutter sharks. Vampire bats appear as **facultative micropredators**. A **parasitoid** is "a parasite that kills its host as a normal and required part of its development", and a **parasitic castrator** "blocks host reproduction" while the host lives ([Lafferty & Kuris 2002](https://parasitology.msi.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/docs/publications/trophic%20strats.pdf)). The "facultative" labels came through a summarising fetch and should be checked against the paper's figure. By the same logic, a hard tick takes one host per life stage without killing it, so it is a typical ectoparasite, not a micropredator. Tick life-stage feeding follows [Tardy et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220245). Parasites also differ from predators in their indirect effects: because they "can consume a host without killing it", they trigger trait-mediated as well as density-mediated effects ([Buck 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pt.2019.07.007)). Their non-consumptive effects on hosts "might overshadow their consumptive effects" ([Buck, Weinstein & Young 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2018.05.001)). For the simulator, this means hematophagy, typical parasitism and parasitoidism are one contact behaviour with four switches: victims per stage, lethality, contact duration (minutes for a mosquito, days for a tick, until host death for a parasitoid) and whether the consumer detaches.

### Mosquito host-seeking is a five-stage cascade with measured distances

Blood-feeder search is the best-parameterised part of this literature, and it is directly boids-compatible. Odour plumes are filaments, not gradients, so "all plume-tracking insects use the wind direction as the primary directional cue" ([Cardé & Willis 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10886-008-9484-5)). Mosquitoes track CO₂ "by surging upwind and casting crosswind". CO₂ then switches on attraction to visual features, and **humans become visible at 5–15 m**. Heat acts only at short range, and each reflex is controlled independently ([van Breugel et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2015.06.046)). Cummins et al. turned this into a spatial model with explicit parameters:

- flight speed 0.4–1.5 m/s, slowing where CO₂ is high;
- CO₂ threshold 0.2 ppm above ambient;
- crosswind legs of 0.5–0.9 s;
- **host contact radius 0.5 m**.

In large-domain runs, crosswind searchers found a host 26.6% of the time, against 4.2% for upwind searchers. The model also reproduced **attack abatement**: tighter host clusters produce narrower plumes, and per-host biting falls in the larger of two equally dense groups ([Cummins et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002500)). A landscape model used a two-stage forager (random flight until a resource is within perception range, then directed flight) on 50 m cells. Field detection distances cited in that paper were about 20–40 m, and daily flights 200–400 m ([Gu & Novak 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.trstmh.2009.01.006)). A self-propelled-particle variant with attractant and repellent terms is already Vicsek-type and slots straight into a boids loop ([Zhou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0244447)). A 3D bed-net model reproduced observed contact times within a few percent for baited nets, 31.5 min modelled against 33.1 min observed ([Jones et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12936-020-03546-5)).

After contact, the feeding cycle supplies the demographic parameters. *Anopheles* blood-feed every 2–4 days; Eckhoff's model uses 3 days, a human blood index of 0.95, about 100 eggs per oviposition and a 10-day adult life expectancy ([Eckhoff 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-10-303)). The same model, the basis of the EMOD vector module, puts the probability that an infectious bite infects a human at about 0.5. Infection raises the mosquito's feeding mortality from 0.1 to 0.15 and cuts its egg batches by a factor of 0.8 ([Eckhoff 2011](https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-2875-10-303)). Blood meals are large relative to the consumer. In seven *Anopheles* species, unfed body weight was 0.76–0.88 of the blood meal volume, which means **a meal of about 1.1–1.3× body mass** ([Phasomkusolsil et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actatropica.2015.09.021)).

Other blood-feeders are less well parameterised:

- **Tabanids** on cattle fed for 2.76 ± 1.77 min and landed 76% of the time on the legs, where tail and head defences do not reach. Hosts stopped reacting after 10–15 s of feeding ([Thinnabut et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.3390/insects15080602)).
- **Vampire bats** take more than half their body weight in blood in about 30 min and starve within 3 days without a meal. They survive this risk through reciprocal food sharing ([Wilkinson, encyclopedia entry](https://science.umd.edu/faculty/wilkinson/Vampire_entry.pdf), [P]; [Carter & Wilkinson 2015](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2015.2524)).
- **Ticks** are modelled as questing ambushers on a daily step. Base host-finding rates were about 0.006 d⁻¹ (nymphs) and 0.009 d⁻¹ (adults), the stages are questing → attached → engorged, and host grooming acts as density-dependent on-host mortality ([Tardy et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220245)). Attachment lasts 3–5 days for larvae, 3–4 for nymphs and 5–7 for adult females, from an extension fact sheet ([UF/IFAS EENY-143](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN300), [P]).

No movement-level model of tsetse, leech or vampire-bat host approach was found.

### Hosts pay mainly in behaviour, and grouping both helps and hurts

Hosts pay for blood-feeders mostly through lost time and extra movement, not lost blood, and this is what couples hematophagy to herd steering. A meta-analysis found that **the intensity of mobile parasites falls as host group size rises, while contagious parasites rise in both prevalence and intensity** ([Côté & Poulin 1995](https://doi.org/10.1093/beheco/6.2.159)). Grouping protects through encounter dilution and through a selfish-herd position effect against flying parasites ([Mooring & Hart 1992](https://ucdavis.pure.elsevier.com/en/publications/animal-grouping-for-protection-from-parasites-selfish-herd-and-en), [P]). Field numbers bear this out:

- **Feral horses.** Larger groups carry fewer flies per head, and harassed horses "bunch tightly together at any group size". Per-capita fly load rises with inter-individual spacing (y = 0.53 + 0.12X), and above 25 km/h of wind landings fall to about 0.33 flies per horse ([Rubenstein & Feinstein 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2021.659570)).
- **Caribou.** The Western Arctic herd moves 121–971 m/h over the year and exceeds 600 m/h only in the peak insect season. Under harassment it forms aggregations of more than 100,000 animals, and animals in the interior are less harassed. Blood loss reaches up to 125 g/day ([Joly et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins12050334)). Oestrid flies and black flies cut feeding time more than mosquitoes do ([Witter et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2656.2011.01905.x)).
- **Tsetse on cattle.** The picture is two-sided. Going from 1 to 12 cattle quadrupled the number of tsetse attracted, and feeding success per fly rose from 54% to 71% because each host defended less. Even so, herding may cut an individual animal's trypanosomiasis risk by up to 90%, and over 89% of meals came from adults ([Torr et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2915.2007.00705.x)).

Non-consumptive avoidance "can be orders of magnitude stronger than consumptive effects" for both predators and parasites ([Buck & Weinstein 2020](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2020.0641)). This "landscape of disgust" is the parasite analogue of the landscape of fear ([Weinstein, Buck & Young 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas8694); content not read, [P]). For the simulator, harassment should feed into host steering in four ways: a cohesion weight that rises with local biter density, centre-seeking, a pull toward upwind or exposed relief habitat, and suppressed foraging speed. Energy should be charged mostly as extra locomotion and lost intake.

### Parasitoids need a kairomone gradient and a death timer

Parasitoid–host systems have their own spatial theory. With Nicholson–Bailey dynamics in each patch and dispersal only to adjacent patches, otherwise unstable host and parasitoid populations persist as spiral waves, spatial chaos or static patterns. "Local movement in a patchy environment can help otherwise unstable host and parasitoid populations persist together" ([Hassell, Comins & May 1991](https://doi.org/10.1038/353255a0)). At the individual level, parasitoids find hosts by chemotaxis up a host-emitted kairomone. The diffusion rate of that kairomone controls patchiness within a generation and stability between generations ([Schofield, Chaplain & Hubbard 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/jtbi.2001.2418)). Females with fewer eggs are choosier: in the field, *Aphytis* females carrying 5.0 eggs oviposited, while those carrying 3.4 host-fed instead ([Heimpel, Rosenheim & Mangel 1996](https://rosenheim.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/137/2014/09/Heimpel-et-al.-1996-Ecology.pdf)). Host discrimination costs inspection time and is not always optimal ([Outreman & Pierre 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.beproc.2005.04.004)). Trophically transmitted parasites add a vulnerability multiplier to the host. Killifish infected by *Euhaplorchis* showed about 4× more conspicuous behaviour and were **10–30× more likely to be eaten by birds**, the parasite's final host. Experimental infections raised conspicuous behaviour 1.8-fold ([Weinersmith et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1645/23-35), reporting [Lafferty & Morris 1996](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/1007991)). Parasites also reshape network structure. They raise link density and connectance, especially through concomitant links, where a predator eats the parasites inside its prey ([Dunne et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1001579)).

## Encounter parameters scale with body mass and the dimension of search space

### Predation: search dimension sets the scaling exponent

Pawar, Dell & Savage gave the cleanest reason to treat flying and swimming agents differently from walking ones. Across 376 species spanning 5×10⁻¹⁴ to 800 kg, consumption rate scales with consumer mass at about **M^0.85 in two-dimensional search** (terrestrial, benthic) and about **M^1.06 in three-dimensional search** (pelagic, aerial, arboreal) ([Pawar et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11131)). A comment paraphrased the 3D prediction as m^1.16 ([Giacomini et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11829)), a figure that probably refers to a different estimate. The commonly quoted component exponents for search rate (about 0.68 in 2D, 1.05 in 3D) were not verified. The researchers' inference follows from search geometry: a ground agent sweeps a strip of width 2r at speed v, so a ≈ 2rv, while a flying or swimming agent sweeps a disc, so a ≈ πr²v. The 3D case therefore gains an extra power of detection radius.

Rall et al. supply regressions that generate attack rate and handling time from consumer mass, resource mass and temperature. Across all 648 data sets, attack rate scaled with consumer mass at about 0.47 and resource mass at 0.15, with an activation energy of 0.44 eV. Handling time scaled at −0.48 and 0.34, with −0.27 eV ([Rall et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0242); values [P], read through a summary). The paper also tabulates separate intercepts and exponents for terrestrial invertebrates, terrestrial endotherms, freshwater and marine vertebrates and invertebrates; units and log base must be checked against the original before use. Kalinkat et al. showed that responses shift gradually from type II, for small predators on similar-sized prey, to type III, for large predators on much smaller prey ([Kalinkat et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.12147)). The FoRAGE database compiles 2,598 standardized functional responses with each interaction's habitat and dimensionality, ready for calibration ([Uiterwaal et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.3706)). Clearance rate and handling time are unimodal in temperature. Consumer and resource masses act independently, so models with separate masses outperform mass-ratio models ([Uiterwaal & DeLong 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.2975)).

### Herbivory: bite size scales as M^0.72, and plant spacing decides whether steering matters

Shipley et al. measured twelve mammal species from 0.05 to 547 kg. **Maximum bite size scaled as M^0.72.** Cropping time was constant at about **0.015 min (0.9 s) per bite**, food processing scaled as M^0.70, and maximum intake rate as M^0.71 ([Shipley et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1086/285648)). Spalinger & Hobbs separated two regimes ([Spalinger & Hobbs 1992](https://doi.org/10.1086/285415), [P]). Where bites are widely spaced, intake is limited by encounter and depends on travel speed and path width: I = v_max·w·D/(1 + δ·w·D). Where bites are dense, intake is limited by chewing and is independent of movement: I = R_max·S/(S + β) ([Fryxell et al., as quoted](https://uknowledge.uky.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=4099&context=igc)). This tells the simulator when steering matters for herbivores. On sparse plants the agent must seek and arrive, and its speed and perception set intake. On dense swards it should slow to a graze state, and its intake is set by bite size.

### Mutualism and floral visitation: saturating benefits and refilling rewards

Mutualistic benefit is not linear in partner density. Net benefit is "likely a monotonically saturating or unimodal function" ([Holland, DeAngelis & Bronstein 2002](https://doi.org/10.1086/338510)). It turns unimodal when partners also consume, as with pollinating seed-eating moths, where too many larvae eat too many ovules ([Holland & DeAngelis 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004420000542)). Cleaner-fish mutualism has hard field numbers. One cleaner wrasse spent 256 min/day inspecting about 2,297 clients, ate 1,218 parasites/day and removed about 0.5 parasites per inspection ([Grutter 1996](https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps/130/m130p061.pdf)). That works out to about 7 s per client visit (the researchers' arithmetic).

For floral visitation, Valdovinos's consumer–resource model gives a full table of mean parameters. Visits are V_ij = α_ij·τ_j·a_j·p_i. Rewards refill as dR/dt = β·p − φ·R − extraction, with β = 0.2, φ = 0.04 and extraction efficiency b = 0.4 per visit. Visit quality σ is the share of a pollinator's visits that go to conspecific plants, and foraging effort adapts at rate G = 2 ([Valdovinos 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/icb/icaf024)). At the individual level, the BEE-STEWARD bumblebee model computes trip duration as distance/velocity plus handling. Handling per flower is (access + inter-flower travel)/patch filling + ingestion, with access time linear in corolla depth. Empirical nectar trips last **18–82 min** ([BEE-STEWARD 2025](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.71468)). The mapping to agents is direct. Each flower holds a nectar standing crop that saturates at β/φ. A visit removes b·R, and it counts as pollination only with probability σ. Adaptive effort α_ij becomes the pollinator's seek weight toward each plant species. Per-flower handling seconds, nectar secretion rates and pollen transfer per visit were not verified this round.

## Aggression runs on one ratio and one intensity parameter

### DomWorld maps onto boids radii almost one-to-one

Hemelrijk's DomWorld is the standard minimal model of aggression, and its structure is already a boids model. Agents avoid others closer than PersSpace, move toward others within NearView, and search up to MaxView. When two agents meet inside personal space they may fight. Agent i wins if Dom_i/(Dom_i + Dom_j) exceeds a uniform random number. Each agent's Dom then shifts by (w_i − expected win) × StepDom, with a floor of 0.01, so unexpected wins move rank more than expected ones. The winner chases about 1 unit and turns 45°; the loser turns 180° and flees about 2 units ([Hemelrijk 1999](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1999.0646); [author PDF](https://www.rug.nl/research/gelifes/tres/_pdf/h99_procroysoc.pdf)). Reported settings, read through an automated summary [P], are:

- radii: PersSpace 2, NearView 24, MaxView 50 units;
- vision angle 120°;
- StepDom for "fierce" (despotic) societies: 0.8 for females, 1.0 for males;
- StepDom for "mild" (egalitarian) societies: 0.1 for females, 0.2 for males.

A later replication used PersSpace 8 rather than 2. It also fitted mangabey StepDom of 0.08–0.10 for adults ([Dolado, Beltran & Quera 2014](https://jasss.soc.surrey.ac.uk/17/1/8.html), [P]), so the original table should be checked.

**A single parameter, StepDom, switches a society between egalitarian and despotic.** Despotic groups also place dominants at the centre ([Hemelrijk 1999](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1999.0646); [Hemelrijk 2002](https://doi.org/10.2307/1543480)). In risk-sensitive variants, an agent attacks only if it would win a "mental battle" against the opponent ([Hemelrijk et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0002678)). The model's predictions are testable: female dominance rising with the proportion of males was confirmed in 9,300 h of data on six wild capuchin groups ([Izar et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0249039)). Extensions add grooming ([Puga-Gonzalez et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000630)) and coalitions ([Hemelrijk & Puga-Gonzalez 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0037271)). Classic contest theory supplies the remaining terms. Resource-holding potential, often body size, sets the prior on Dom ([Parker 1974](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5193(74)90111-8)). The hawk–dove game gives an attack probability of V/C when injury cost C exceeds resource value V ([Maynard Smith & Price 1973](https://www.nature.com/articles/246015a0), [P]).

### Territories, interference and kleptoparasitism need fields and thresholds, not new physics

Territoriality emerges from scent marks and retreat, with no explicit boundaries. In Giuggioli's territorial random walk, every agent marks each site it visits. Marks stay active for a time T_AS, and an agent that meets foreign active scent retreats. For Bristol red foxes, the 3–4-day lag before neighbours took over a dead resident's territory matched the model's T_AS, provided foxes patrolled their borders ([Giuggioli, Potts & Harris 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002008)). Adding attraction to a den stabilises home ranges. Step-selection functions are formally equivalent to these mechanistic movement kernels ([Potts & Lewis 2014](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2014.0231)). Coyote territories in Yellowstone are explained by prey distribution plus "an avoidance response to the presence of neighbouring packs" ([Moorcroft, Lewis & Crabtree 2006](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2005.3439)). Memory gives the decay constants for remembered avoidance: roe deer use attribute memory with a half-life of 0.9 d and spatial memory with a half-life of 5.6 d ([Ranc et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2014856118)).

At food patches, interference has a geometric closed form. If an agent is attacked whenever a competitor lies within attack distance D_A, then **P(attack) = 1 − exp(−πρD_A²)**, and intake falls roughly exponentially with competitor density ρ ([Rappoldt, Stillman & Ens 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304380009006607), [P]). Behaviour-based shorebird IBMs built on such rules predicted a 3.65% rise in winter mortality after habitat loss, against 3.17% observed ([Goss-Custard et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1890/1051-0761(2006)016[2215:toabim]2.0.co;2)). Kleptoparasitism has a clean evolutionarily stable rule: challenge a food handler when finding food yourself is slower than waiting out its handling time, i.e. when ν_f·f·t_h < 1 ([Broom & Ruxton 1998](https://www.vliz.be/imisdocs/publications/ocrd/133703.pdf), [P]; the threshold was reconstructed and checked against the paper's worked example).

### Mobbing switches the neighbour rule, and interspecific aggression is common

Jackdaws show that aggression can change the flocking rule itself. Transit flocks interact topologically with 7–8 neighbours. Mobbing flocks switch to metric interactions within about 5 m (about 14 body lengths), and their order rises with density. The fitted 3D model used:

- speed 10 m/s;
- interaction radius 4 m and repulsion radius 0.5 m;
- a harmonic pull toward the predator of strength 0.04;
- noise 0.05–0.15 ([Ling et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13281-4)).

A review of 337 mobbing papers found heterospecific eavesdropping on mobbing calls to be "widely prevalent", with minimum approach distances from under 1 m to about 4 m ([Carlson & Griesser, preprint](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/3921/download/7743/), [P]). Interspecific aggression is ordinary rather than exceptional:

- In birds, interspecific territoriality is widespread and associated with hybridization and breeding-season resource overlap ([Drury, Cowen & Grether 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1921380117)).
- In damselflies, an agonistic-character-displacement model fitted with field interference data matched 80% of stable outcomes across 32 populations ([Grether et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.13395)).
- Grether and colleagues unify aggressive and reproductive interference as drivers of coexistence and range limits ([Grether et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2017.07.004)).

An `aggressorOf` edge between species with similar signals and overlapping resources is therefore biologically justified, not decoration.

## Dispersal, epibiosis and attached parasites are cargo with a timer

### Movement, not gut passage, sets most seed-dispersal kernels

Mechanistic seed-dispersal models agree that kernels should emerge rather than be imposed, and that the animal's movement dominates. In Morales & Carlo's simulation, plant aggregation mainly set the scale of the dispersal kernel and frugivore density mainly set its shape ([Morales & Carlo 2006](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658(2006)87[1489:teopda]2.0.co;2)). On Saipan, differences in dispersal between bird species "were largely driven by interspecific variation in bird movement rather than … gut passage times" ([Rehm et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2018.2007), [P]). In the SEADS model, movement distance had the largest effect size on dispersal distance, 0.83. Gut retention there was gamma-distributed (shape 4, scale 5) and rescaled to means of 15–480 min ([Jones et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3113)). That paper's literature table gives a ready parameter set. Songbirds of 30–119 g retain seeds 17–135 min and disperse them 50–1,168 m. Monkeys retain seeds 376–1,180 min and disperse them 317–327 m. An elephant retains them 2,370 min and disperses them 1,522 m ([Jones et al. 2017, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5528214/)). The researchers fitted that table's endotherm rows themselves, as a rough heuristic rather than a published allometry: GRT ≈ 117·M^0.43 min (M in kg; r² = 0.76, n = 16) and dispersal distance ≈ 237·M^0.20 m (r² = 0.35).

Deposition is clumped, not diffuse. Spider monkeys moved about half their dispersed seeds in transit and half to sleeping sites, mostly reused ones. Mean dispersal distance was 245 m for dispersed seeds, and a mixture kernel always beat single lognormal, Weibull or 2Dt kernels ([Russo, Portnoy & Augspurger 2006](https://russolab.unl.edu/PDF/Russo_et_al_2006_Ecol.pdf), [P]). Mallards commute between day roosts and night foraging sites ([Kleyheeg et al. 2017, Mov Ecol](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40462-016-0092-7)). About 34% of the seeds they disperse end up at roosts, and seed survival matters more than retention time ([Kleyheeg et al. 2017, J Ecol](https://pure.knaw.nl/portal/en/publications/seed-dispersal-distributions-resulting-from-landscape-dependent-d/), [P]). Their retention curves are gamma with shape about 2.7, and median migratory dispersal distances reach 293–413 km ([Kleyheeg et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2019.00040/full), [P]). Small, hard seeds survive digestion best ([Kleyheeg, Claessens & Soons 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0195026); [Lovas-Kiss et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.5997)).

Other vectors need only a different carry rule:

- **Fish.** Tambaqui disperse seeds a modelled mean of 337–552 m and up to 5.5 km, and larger fish carry them farther ([Anderson et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2011.0155)).
- **Ants.** Myrmecochory averages 2.24 m, with a maximum of about 180 m ([Yun et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13410411/), [P]).
- **Ungulate fur (epizoochory).** Retention half-lives depend on species and body zone: on red deer, 1 min on the head but more than 6 h on the rump. Grooming caused 7–14% of detachments, and 5% of diaspores passed between animals ([epizoochory study, Ecol Evol 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3768); first author unverified).

The stakes are global. Half of all plant species depend on animals for seed dispersal, and losing mammal and bird dispersers has already cut plants' capacity to track climate change by about 60% ([Fricke et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abk3510)). In tropical regrowth plots, the least dispersal disruption went with about 4× faster carbon accumulation ([Fricke et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2500951122)). Seed dispersal effectiveness is quantity × quality ([Schupp, Jordano & Gómez 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-8137.2010.03402.x)), so deposition must feed a microsite-dependent establishment rule. Long-distance dispersal is driven mainly by large and migratory animals ([Nathan et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2008.08.003)).

### Epibionts grow, detach and impose drag; phoronts ride between resources

Wahl frames epibiosis as an "ecological lever". Epibionts change a host's surface properties (drag, light and nutrient exchange, predation risk) and so "greatly amplify or buffer" the stresses the host experiences ([Wahl 2008](https://doi.org/10.1080/08927010802339772)). The best agent-level numbers come from sea-turtle barnacles. *Chelonibia testudinaria* on green turtles follows von Bertalanffy growth with k = 0.0091 d⁻¹ and L∞ = 59.5 mm, and settles about 76 days before nesting. **24.1% of barnacles detached during a single 9–21-day inter-nesting interval**, and host identity explained 66.4% of growth variance ([Poyatos et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-025-04683-8)). Assuming a constant hazard, that implies a detachment rate of about 0.013–0.031 d⁻¹ (the researchers' arithmetic). Adult barnacles even crawl up to 78.6 mm a year, mostly against the water flow ([Chan et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2021.1620)). Loads can be extreme: debilitated juvenile loggerheads carried 1,092 ± 540 barnacles against 139 ± 189 on healthy turtles, and "the epibiont load may double the volume and mass" of a juvenile. Whether sickness causes heavy fouling or the reverse is unresolved ([Baruffaldi et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2023.1243153)).

On whales, epibiont occurrence rises modestly with host length. Epibiont survival depends on where the host travels: Antarctic minke whales carried no recruiting barnacles, and *Xenobalanus* died where sea-surface temperature fell below 12 °C ([Ten et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-25929-1)). A 2026 study coupled a morbillivirus SIR model of striped dolphins with mechanistic epibiont population models. Host crashes cascaded into host-specific epibionts but not generalists ([Ten et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.70216)). This is the closest published analogue to host-density-driven epibiont dynamics. **No primary measurement of the drag or energy cost of barnacles on turtles or whales was found.** The researchers derived an upper-bound scenario from drag physics, not from an epibiont study. At constant propulsive power, speed scales as (C_d·A)^(−1/3), so doubling effective drag area cuts cruise speed by about 21%.

Epiphytes on trees behave as a metapopulation in which trees are patches that die. Colonisation depends on connectivity to occupied trees and on tree size; extinction is driven by tree fall ([Snäll et al. 2005](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/04-0531), [P]; [Johansson, Ranius & Snäll 2012](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/11-0760.1), [P]). Epiphyte–tree networks are predicted by host abundance, spatial overlap, host size, wood density and bark roughness ([Sáyago et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2012.2821)). They are more nested but less specialised and less modular than pollination or seed-dispersal networks ([Naranjo et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/aobpla/plz011)), and that pattern can serve as a validation target. Phoresy, transport on another animal, is recorded in at least 13 phyla, 25 classes and 60 orders. Phoronts are typically small and of low mobility and use ephemeral resources, many are also parasites, and how they attach and detach is "largely unknown" ([Bartlow & Agosta 2021](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.12654)). No quantitative attach or detach probabilities were found, so phoresy must reuse the epizoochory machinery with resource-quality triggers.

## Nesting and oviposition are occupancy decisions with delayed recruitment

### Cavities are a persistent, limiting resource built by a few engineers

Nest webs link trees, excavators such as woodpeckers, and non-excavating secondary cavity nesters ([Ibarra et al. 2020](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol25/iss2/art27/)). They give the nesting interaction a measurable population consequence. Cavities in living trees persist "for several decades" and are reused more than 20 times. Excavator entrance areas range from 3.1 cm² (a 12 g piculet) to 225 cm² (a 260 g woodpecker). In logged forest the researchers found nine times fewer cavities and 17 times fewer nests than in old growth ([Ibarra et al. 2020](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol25/iss2/art27/)). A 16-year study of 1,732 cavities in British Columbia showed that **secondary-nester density tracks excavator nesting density in the previous year**. Excavators leave a legacy because cavities accumulate faster than they decay ([Trzcinski et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.13626)). A commentary called this "the most compelling empirical evidence to date" of nest-site limitation, while warning that North American conifer forests, where most holes are excavated, may not represent forests where decay supplies most cavities ([Walters 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.13704)). In South American *Araucaria* forests, decay-formed cavities in broadleaf trees dominate ([Cockle et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10531-019-01826-4)). Nest webs rewire. During a beetle outbreak, cavity supply peaked and 74% of links persisted from year to year ([Cockle & Martin 2015](https://doi.org/10.1890/14-1256.1)). That gives about 26% annual link turnover as a validation pattern.

Nest-site choice can be implemented as habitat filling in which a site's payoff falls as it fills. In the despotic version, residents lower the suitability seen by later settlers ([Fretwell & Lucas 1970](https://www.scirp.org/reference/referencespapers?referenceid=710787), [P]). Risk cues modify the choice. Pied flycatchers avoid nest-site features linked with heterospecific nest failure ([Tolvanen et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12862-018-1301-3)). Daurian redstarts nest closer to buildings when cuckoo parasitism risk is high ([Zhang et al. 2023, Curr Biol](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.01.047)). Breeding timers can be drawn per species from the Amniote Life-History Database. It holds incubation and fledging ages and clutch sizes for 9,802 birds, 4,953 mammals and 6,567 reptiles ([Myhrvold et al. 2015](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/15-0846R.1)).

### Collective nesters switch modes at a quorum

Honeybee swarms and *Temnothorax* ants choose nest sites by quorum, a rule that maps directly onto agents. In honeybees, **10–15 or more scouts at one site** reliably preceded worker piping, the signal to prepare for lift-off, which came about an hour later. The whole process from discovery to lift-off took about 2.5–4.5 h ([Seeley & Visscher 2003](https://bees.ucr.edu/sites/default/files/2020-06/bes54.pdf); [Seeley & Visscher 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-004-0814-5)). Bees prefer cavities of about 40 L, entrances of about 12.5 cm² near the bottom, sites at 5 m height, and sites more than 300 m from the parent colony ([Seeley & Morse 1978](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02224297)). Passino & Seeley showed that the quorum threshold and the decay of dance advertising together tune the trade-off between decision speed and accuracy ([Passino & Seeley 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-005-0067-y)). Reina et al. give a design pattern for implementing the rule in multi-agent systems ([Reina et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0140950)). Ants estimate the quorum from the rate at which they meet nestmates; detection takes about 2 min, and smaller nests reach quorum at lower populations ([Pratt 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/beheco/v16y2005i2p488-496.html)). Reaching quorum switches recruitment from slow tandem runs to carrying, which is three times faster ([Franks et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2008.0224)). Masuda et al. modelled ants with individual acceptance thresholds, using N = 100 and a quorum of 0.5 N ([Masuda et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.140533)). NetLogo's BeeSmart model implements the whole scout → dance → quorum → swarm sequence ([Guo & Wilensky 2014](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/netlogo/models/BeeSmartHiveFinding)).

### Egg load drives choosiness, and preference does not always match offspring success

Oviposition choice is a preference filtered through internal state. On average, herbivorous insects lay more eggs where their offspring perform better, and the match is stronger in specialists ([Gripenberg et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2009.01433.x)). Egg load is the key state variable: as it falls, females become more selective ([Heimpel, Rosenheim & Mangel 1996](https://rosenheim.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/137/2014/09/Heimpel-et-al.-1996-Ecology.pdf)). Container mosquitoes break the preference–performance rule. In Iquitos, *Aedes aegypti* laid more eggs in large, unmanaged containers, but preference correlated *negatively* with pupation probability because larvae compete there ([Wong et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pntd.0001632)). *Aedes aegypti* also practises skip oviposition, spreading a few eggs over many sites, and this drives its dispersal ([Reiter 2007](https://doi.org/10.1089/vbz.2006.0630)). Butterflies respond to plant isolation at fine scale. Cabbage whites put more eggs on isolated plants at 1 m spacing, but the effect faded by 6–36 m ([Hasenbank & Hartley 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00442-014-3103-7)). Priority effects matter in water: treefrogs and mosquitoes laid fewer eggs in pools that already held predatory beetles ([Pintar & Resetarits 2020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.6845)).

Aquatic insects find water by its horizontally polarised reflection, and this makes some architectural surfaces into **ecological traps**. Mayflies at a lit Danube bridge laid eggs on asphalt ([Szaz et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121194)). Caddisflies are drawn to dark glass ([Kriska et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-008-0345-4)). Twenty-seven tabanid species show positive polarotaxis ([Horváth et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-008-0425-5)). For a Rhino-based simulator this is a direct link between designed geometry and population outcomes. For fish, the inSALMO model fully specifies spawning:

- a daily spawn probability of 0.2 within a temperature window of 5–14 °C;
- redd cells chosen by a random draw weighted by the area of available gravel, minus the area defended by guarding females;
- a redd area of 5.6 m² and a defence area of 20 m² per guarding female ([inSALMO 7](https://www.humboldt.edu/sites/default/files/ecological-modeling/2024-09/insalmo-72description2021-05-06.pdf)).

Brood parasites are the fast end of oviposition. Common cuckoos laid in 2.6–26 s (mean 7.3 s), usually in the afternoon, and removed 0–2 host eggs ([Wang et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.24272/j.issn.2095-8137.2020.021)). They chose nests whose eggs matched their own ([Zhang et al. 2023, Proc R Soc B](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2022.2094)). Multiparasitism, more than one parasite egg in a host nest, is widespread and costly to both parties ([Soler 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/brv.70018)).

## Allelopathy works as a diffusing field read through a hormetic curve

### The diffusion length decides whether chemistry maintains or destroys diversity

Allelopathy models fall into three families, and all three can share one grid field. The population-level form adds a product term to Lotka–Volterra competition. The term is proportional to N₁·N₂: a negative coefficient gives inhibition and a positive one gives stimulation. Kumawat et al. give a stimulatory version with illustrative coefficients, such as σ = 0.003 ([Kumawat et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-70596-z)). They credit Maynard Smith and Chattopadhyay ([Chattopadhyay 1996](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3800(94)00134-0)). Stimulatory allelopathy with a time delay produces stable limit cycles ([Mukhopadhyay et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0025-5564(98)00005-4)). Toxin production lets two phytoplankton species coexist on one resource above a threshold ([Roy 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tpb.2008.11.003)). Including autotoxicity lets a producer limit itself ([Kengwoung-Keumo 2016](https://doi.org/10.3934/mbe.2016018)).

In lattice models, a susceptible plant's death rate rises with the local density of the allelopath, h = 1 + γ·f ([Lanchier & Mercer 2023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17016), preprint). In hybrid cellular automata with a diffusing allelochemical, **all species coexist at low diffusion, mass extinction follows at high diffusion, and diversity-rich Turing-like patterns appear at intermediate diffusion** ([Carvalho, Mota & Martins 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13020512)). The microbial rock–paper–scissors experiment points the same way: local interaction keeps all three strains, while global mixing loses them ([Kerr et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature00823)). The design consequence follows. **The diffusion length L = √(D/λ) relative to plant spacing is the main tuning knob**, and a field that spreads too far behaves like global mixing.

Some allelopathy acts only on contact. About 40–70% of common seaweeds bleach and kill coral tissue, but only where they touch it. Their lipid-soluble extracts do the same, and herbivorous fish limit how often contacts occur by grazing the seaweed ([Rasher & Hay 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0912095107)). For aquatic plants and algae, this means a footprint-overlap rule rather than a field. It also creates a trophic–non-trophic coupling: herbivores indirectly protect corals. The novel-weapons hypothesis motivated much invasion research. *Centaurea diffusa* harms North American grasses more than grasses from its native range ([Callaway & Aschehoug 2000](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.290.5491.521)). But a 2025 systematic review found "confused definitions and insufficient empirical evidence to support the NWH" ([Colautti & Antunes 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.70415)), and the catechin evidence conflicts ([Bais & Kaushik 2010](https://doi.org/10.4161/cib.3.5.12559); [Bais et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.4161/psb.5.3.10573)). Novel weapons belong in the simulator as a scenario, not a default.

### Positive and negative allelopathy are two ends of one dose–response curve

Hormesis supplies the positive arm. The Brain–Cousens model, implemented in R's drc package, adds a hormesis term f to the log-logistic curve: f(x) = c + (d − c + f·x)/(1 + exp(b·(log x − log e))) ([drc braincousens](https://doseresponse.github.io/drc/reference/braincousens.html)). Measured stimulation is modest:

- **9–16% above control in relative growth rate** and **38–43% in dry weight** across 687 curves for plants and algae ([Cedergreen et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.2203/dose-response.06-008.Cedergreen));
- up to **138% of control in leaf area** for the allelochemical parthenin, with ED50 0.62 kg/ha and ED90 1.81 kg/ha. Stimulating doses were inhibitory at first, so hormesis depends on time ([Belz 2008](https://doi.org/10.2203/dose-response.07-007.Belz)).

Density matters too. Phytochemicals block germination at low seed density but can raise it at high density, because the toxin is shared among more receivers ([Sinkkonen 2005](https://doi.org/10.2201/nonlin.003.02.004)). Decay rates are available for one chemical family. In soil, DIBOA degrades to BOA with a half-life of 43 h, BOA degrades to APO with a half-life of 2.5 d, and APO was not degraded after 3 months ([Macías et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf048702l)). A two-layer field with a fast parent compound and a persistent product therefore reproduces "soil sickness" legacies.

Resource-mediated facilitation should be kept distinct from chemical stimulation. In a dryland model, nurse plants raise water infiltration as I(n) = (βn + i₀)/(βn + 1) and give protégés grazing protection of up to μ = 0.2–0.9. The stress-gradient hypothesis then emerges from the mechanism rather than being imposed ([Díaz-Sierra et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-52447-z)). **No sourced values exist for allelochemical release rates or diffusion coefficients in soil or water.** These must be calibration parameters.

## Herds move by contagious state switches, not continuous flocking

### Sheep are the best-parameterised mammal herd

Land-mammal herds do not flock continuously the way starlings do. They alternate between dispersed grazing and bursts of collective movement, and the switch between states is socially contagious. Ginelli et al. tracked 100 Merino sheep in an 80 × 80 m field. They found slow dispersion punctuated by "fast, avalanche-like regrouping events distributed on all experimentally accessible scales" ([Ginelli et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26417082/)). Their model has three states: static/grazing (v = 0), walking (v = 0.15 m/s) and running (v = 1.5 m/s). It uses the following parameters:

- switching times τ₀→₁ = 35 s and τ₁→₀ = 8 s;
- allelomimetic strength α = 15 and exponent δ = 4;
- stopping distance 6.3 m, recruitment distance 31.6 m and metric alignment range 1 m.

Walkers align with metric neighbours. Running decisions use the first shell of Voronoi neighbours, and runners align only with other runners. Herd density ranged from 0.04 sheep/m² when dispersed to 1.3/m² after a regrouping event ([Ginelli et al. 2015, author PDF](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/files/58184052/PNAS_2015_Ginelli_12729_34.pdf)). The exact rate functions were reconstructed from the paper, not quoted verbatim, and should be checked against its Methods.

Two independent experiments give the same portable rule. When a trained ewe initiated departure, followers left at rate μ = α·D^β/S^γ, where D is the number already departed and S the number remaining. The fitted values were α = 0.19 s⁻¹, β = 1.16 and γ = 0.6 ([Pillot et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21245930/)). A stop-and-go model had the same structure: the start rate depends on walkers ahead and the stop rate on stopped animals behind. It produced a travelling sech² density pulse ([Azaïs et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30517114/); parameters from the [arXiv version](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1712.05774), [P]). **A switching rate proportional to (neighbours in the target state)^β / (neighbours not in it)^γ, with β ≈ 0.6–1.2 and γ ≈ 0.5–0.7, is therefore a general contagion rule for ungulate activity.** Sheep groups of 2–4 move single file in a hierarchical, directed and topological network, with a new random leader each episode. Moving and grazing phases are separated at 0.45 m/s ([Gómez-Nava, Bon & Peruani 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-022-01769-8)). Under threat, sheep show "a strong attraction towards the centre of the flock" ([King et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22835787/)). When herded by a border collie, 14 ewes moved at about 1.3 m/s with a cohesion radius of 1.21 m and polarisation of 0.85. The dog stayed 3–7 m from the flock's centre ([Jadhav et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-07245-8)). Vigilance and cortisol both fall as group size rises from 2 to 100 ([Michelena et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22496078/)), although a 2026 re-examination calls the general group-size effect on vigilance "highly inconsistent" ([Verdolin & Red 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-026-03800-x), [P]).

### Wild ungulates and primates weight neighbours by distance and share leadership

Migrating caribou follow neither a purely metric nor a purely topological rule. In drone footage, a model with **exponentially decaying neighbour weights** beat both alternatives. Neighbours concentrated about 2 m in front and behind, so caribou travel in lines. Calves relied more on social cues and large bulls less ([Torney et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29581404/)). Baboons show how decisions are shared:

- Neither rank nor sex predicted whether an initiator was followed ([Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26089514/)).
- When two initiators pulled in directions more than about 90° apart, followers chose one, usually the majority; below 90° they compromised ([Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26089514/)).
- Movement is best predicted by **4–6 neighbours** ([Farine et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27292778/)).
- The strongest single predictor is a preference for ground other troop members recently crossed ([Strandburg-Peshkin et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28139196/)).

Feral horses show limited distributed leadership: any member can initiate, but only alpha males herd from behind ([Krueger et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24220794/)). Przewalski's horses form multilevel societies whose movement similarity predicts future harem membership ([Ozogány et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37669934/)). Herds that mix fast and slow animals split into fast and slow subgroups ([Gueron, Levin & Rubenstein 1996](https://scielo.org.za/pdf/sajs/v104n5-6/a0910406.pdf), [P]). Differences in activity budgets also explain sexual segregation, in 22 of 23 studies ([Ruckstuhl & Neuhaus 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11911375/)). Segregation should therefore emerge from class-specific switching rates without an explicit rule. Solitary rodents need no flocking rule: a correlated random walk with forage and travel states and a home-range attractor is enough. Bat colonies emerge as dense streams at 9–15 m/s, peaking at about 36,000 bats/min ([Betke et al. 2008](https://acoustics.mit.edu/faculty/makris/JoMammalogy_89.1_2008_betke.pdf), [P]), and bats avoid collisions with collision-cone rules ([Brace et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27655669/)).

## Insects on land and water show density thresholds and quorum responses

### Marching insects order above a critical density

Wingless locust nymphs move in a disordered way below about 17 per m². Between about 25 and 62 per m² they are ordered but switch direction spontaneously, and from about 74 per m² they stay ordered. Field bands average about 50 per m² ([Buhl et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16741126/); density values [P], via a summary). Their fitted self-propelled-particle model used a speed of 1.9 cm/s and an interaction range of 13.9 cm ([Buhl et al. 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/7042094_From_Disorder_to_Order_in_Marching_Locusts), [P]). The driver is cannibalism. Nymphs that cannot sense others approaching from behind start moving less often, and cannibalism rises ([Bazazi et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18472424/)). Escape from conspecifics behind plus pursuit of those ahead is enough to produce collective motion with no alignment term ([Romanczuk, Couzin & Schimansky-Geier 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19257176/)). Individuals move in a pause-and-go rhythm: the mean pause is 2.3 s with a heavy tail, and 54% of walking bouts start after a touch ([Ariel et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24988464/)). Mormon crickets are on a "forced march for protein and salt", and crickets that stop are eaten ([Simpson et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16537500/)). Processionary caterpillars follow the one ahead in head-to-tail trains. *Ochrogaster lunifer* travels at 17.4 m/h and covers about 40 m/day ([Uemura et al. 2020](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-020-0189-x), [P]). Cockroaches aggregate without leaders, because each stays longer under shelters that hold more conspecifics ([Amé et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16581903/)).

### Ants follow fields, and their traffic does not jam

Army ants form lanes when outbound and returning ants turn at different rates. Couzin & Franks's model used:

- body length 0.8 cm;
- an avoidance zone of 0.4 cm and a perception zone of 1.2 cm;
- a desired speed of 13 cm/s;
- an avoidance turning rate of 500°/s;
- pheromone diffusion of 0.01 cm²/s ([Couzin & Franks 2003](https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/couzin.pdf), [P]).

At a fork, the choice follows Deneubourg's function P_A = (A + k)^n / [(A + k)^n + (B + k)^n], with k ≈ 20 and n ≈ 2 ([Deneubourg et al. 1990, via review](https://coderanger.net/svn/school/2006/fall/ai/aco/docs/Ant%20Algorithms%20for%20Discrete%20Optimization.pdf), [P]). Under crowding, a second trail forms before flow drops ([Dussutour et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14999281/)). Flow then plateaus with density instead of collapsing ([Poissonnier et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31635695/)).

### Surface insects space by ripples and expand by quorum

Whirligig beetles form dense rafts alongside a dilute phase. Speed falls with local density as v ∝ ρ^−0.4. The free speed is about 13 body lengths per second (about 16 cm/s), and alignment lags by about 13 ms ([Devereux et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33849331/)). Bursts reach 100 body lengths per second ([Sun et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38194918/)). Vision drives attraction, and surface-wave sensing through the antennae drives short-range repulsion ([Romey, Miller & Vidal 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24943378/)). **Flash expansion is a quorum response**: in groups of 24, one or two beetles that could see the threat were not enough to trigger it, but four usually were ([Romey & Lamb 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26301958/)). Water striders keep their spacing with Gaussian short-range repulsion and weak long-range attraction, and they show "waves of fear" moving away from shore disturbances. The model's nearest-neighbour distribution matched field data (KS p = 0.84) ([Kovalev, Filippov & Gorb 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34508112/)).

### Flying-insect gaps close with spring-tethered swarms and streaker guides

Mosquito mating swarms behave like damped springs tethered to the swarm centre. The fitted natural frequencies are about 0.8–1.0 Hz horizontally (damping ratio 0.48) and 0.41 Hz vertically (damping ratio 1.4). Nearest-neighbour distance is 13 cm, and alignment is weak: mean heading disagreement with neighbours was about 75°, against 84° for random headings ([Butail et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23802449/)). Individuals fly at 1–4 m/s ([Butail et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22628212/)). Honeybee swarms are steered by fewer than 5% informed scouts. These "streakers" fly fast toward the new site ([Schultz, Passino & Seeley 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18840663/)). The swarm as a whole moved at about 0.33 m/s, and scouts reached up to 1.6 m/s ([Greggers et al. 2013](https://honeybee.neurobiologie.fu-berlin.de.mirror.hiveeyes.org/column/paper/Scouts_behave_as_streakers_in_honeybee_swarms.pdf), [P]). Mayfly emergences are pulses: one emergence put 87.9 billion insects, 3,078 t of biomass, into the air within hours ([Stepanian et al. 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31964842/)). Polarotaxis keeps mayfly swarms over water ([Farkas et al. 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27178399/)).

Waterbirds close the remaining bird gap. Ibis in V-formation take aerodynamically predicted positions and phase their wingbeats to exploit upwash ([Portugal et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24429637/)), and pairs swap the lead reciprocally ([Voelkl et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25646487/)). Emperor penguin huddles pack at 21 birds per m². Every 30–60 s, travelling waves of 5–10 cm steps pass through them ([Zitterbart et al. 2011](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0020260)), and any bird can trigger a wave once its gap exceeds about 2 cm ([Gerum et al. 2013](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1367-2630/15/12/125022), [P]). Huddling switches on like a phase transition in apparent temperature ([Richter et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30416209/)).

## Design guidance for the Grasshopper ecosystem simulator

This section is the researchers' synthesis. It turns the evidence above into build decisions for a Rhino 8 simulator with flying, land and aquatic animals, plants, trees and algae. It builds on the earlier round's design, which kept an interaction-outcome matrix separate from a steering matrix, gave each taxon its own neighbour rule, and used per-agent state machines and allometric energy budgets. A separate interaction-schema document is being written, so field-level definitions are left to it.

### Build five reusable mechanisms, then parameterise twelve interactions

Each of the five mechanisms should be one code module, written in C# or Python in the Rhino 8 Script component. Each interaction type then becomes a parameter set that the module reads:

1. **ConsumerContact** reads lethality (none, required, castrates), victims per stage, contact duration, detach-after-feed, biomass transferred as a fraction of consumer or victim mass, and an optional pathogen. Predation, herbivory, hematophagy, parasitism, parasitoidism, floral visitation and cleaning mutualism are all settings of it. Lafferty & Kuris's four dichotomies define the switches ([Lafferty & Kuris 2002](https://parasitology.msi.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/docs/publications/trophic%20strats.pdf)).
2. **Contest** reads Dom (seeded from body mass), StepDom, attack radius, chase and flee impulses, fight duration and cost. Territoriality and mobbing add a scent field or a recruitment broadcast ([Hemelrijk 1999](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.1999.0646); [Giuggioli et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002008); [Ling et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13281-4)).
3. **Cargo** is a list on every mobile agent. Each item records its origin, pickup time, an exit timer or hazard, a survival probability and a growth model. Seeds, burrs, phoronts, barnacles, attached ticks and pollen loads all use it ([Jones et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.3113); [Poyatos et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-025-04683-8); [Tardy et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220245)).
4. **Occupancy** is a registry of substrates: trees, cavities, branches, leaves, water bodies, host nests, host bodies and designed Rhino geometry. Each has a quality, a capacity, occupants, an egg cohort list and a lifetime. Agents seek substrates only in the right reproductive state. A quorum switch serves colonial nesters ([Seeley & Visscher 2004](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-004-0814-5); [Trzcinski et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2656.13626)).
5. **Field** is a grid or mesh scalar with emission, first-order decay (λ = ln2/half-life), diffusion and an optional transformation into a second product. Allelochemicals, territorial scent, ant pheromone, baboon "recently traversed" trails, host odour plumes and kairomones all use it ([Carvalho et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13020512); [Couzin & Franks 2003](https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/couzin.pdf); [Schofield et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1006/jtbi.2001.2418)).

Each successful event should be logged as a typed edge with a counter: GloBI label, RO IRI where one exists, sign, and Kéfi class. The simulator then rebuilds an emergent quantitative network that can be compared with empirical nest webs, oviposition webs and pollination webs.

### Generate encounter parameters from body mass and search dimension

Do not hand-tune attack and handling rates species by species. Generate them from consumer mass, resource mass and temperature with Rall et al.'s group regressions, after checking the original table ([Rall et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2012.0242)). Then give 3D searchers (flying birds and insects, fish, swimming birds) spherical detection volumes and 2D searchers (land mammals, walking insects, surface insects) disc sensors. The steeper 3D mass scaling then emerges from geometry ([Pawar et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11131)). Switch to a type III response (Hill exponent of about 1.2–2) when predator mass is much larger than prey mass ([Kalinkat et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.12147)). For herbivores, bite size ∝ M^0.72 and about 0.9 s per bite set the biomass removed from a plant agent per tick ([Shipley et al. 1994](https://doi.org/10.1086/285648)). Steering speed should matter only when plants are sparse ([Spalinger & Hobbs 1992](https://doi.org/10.1086/285415)). For flowers, store a nectar standing crop that refills toward β/φ. Each visit removes about 40% of it, and a visit counts as pollination only with probability σ, the share of conspecific pollen the visitor carries ([Valdovinos 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/icb/icaf024)).

### Use one contagious state-switch engine for every herd and colony

A single rate function, r = r₀·(1 + α·n_target)^β / (1 + n_other)^γ over metric or Voronoi neighbours, covers every activity switch in this report:

- graze ↔ walk ↔ run in sheep ([Ginelli et al. 2015](https://abdn.elsevierpure.com/files/58184052/PNAS_2015_Ginelli_12729_34.pdf); [Pillot et al. 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21245930/));
- rest ↔ leave in cockroaches ([Amé et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16581903/));
- pause ↔ go in locusts ([Ariel et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24988464/));
- raft ↔ flash expansion in whirligigs ([Romey & Lamb 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26301958/));
- scout ↔ commit in bees and ants ([Pratt 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/beheco/v16y2005i2p488-496.html)).

Quorum responses are the limit of large β. Give each sex and age class its own base rates, so that sexual segregation and fast/slow fragmentation emerge rather than being imposed ([Ruckstuhl & Neuhaus 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11911375/)). Couple biting-insect density to herd steering: higher cohesion, centre-seeking, a pull toward relief habitat, and suppressed foraging. The tighter spacing then lowers per-capita bites, as horses and caribou show ([Rubenstein & Feinstein 2021](https://doi.org/10.3389/fevo.2021.659570); [Joly et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/toxins12050334)).

### Treat fields as the main tuning knobs for plants, algae and chemistry

Plants, trees and algae should interact with each other mainly through fields and footprints, not steering. The allelochemical diffusion length L = √(D/λ) should start at about 1–2 times the emitter's zone-of-influence radius and be exposed to the user. The evidence says diversity collapses when L is large relative to interaction scales ([Carvalho et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/life13020512); [Kerr et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature00823)). Each receiver should read a running average of exposure through a normalised Brain–Cousens curve, R(C) = (1 + f·C)/(1 + (C/e)^b). The researchers calculated that b = 2 with f between 0.8 and 1.5 gives peak stimulation of about 1.14–1.40, which matches the measured range of hormesis ([Cedergreen et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.2203/dose-response.06-008.Cedergreen); [Belz 2008](https://doi.org/10.2203/dose-response.07-007.Belz)). Use contact-only allelopathy for seaweed on coral, so that grazing fish indirectly protect reefs ([Rasher & Hay 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0912095107)). For a stable explicit 2D update, keep D·Δt/Δx² ≤ 0.25.

Trees should carry habitat slots for cavities and epiphytes. Their death should remove or drop everything they host, which gives epiphyte metapopulations their extinction term and nest webs their decay term ([Snäll et al. 2005](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1890/04-0531); [Ibarra et al. 2020](https://www.ecologyandsociety.org/vol25/iss2/art27/)). Designed surfaces that are dark, smooth and horizontally polarising should register as oviposition attractors for aquatic insects. This makes ecological traps visible in the design model ([Szaz et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121194); [Horváth et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00114-008-0425-5)).

### Run four clocks, not one

Collective motion and the new interactions run on very different time scales:

- **About 0.02 s:** ants and whirligigs ([Couzin & Franks 2003](https://www.sccs.swarthmore.edu/users/08/bblonder/phys120/docs/couzin.pdf); [Devereux et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33849331/)).
- **About 0.05 s:** bird and insect flight ([Ling et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13281-4)).
- **About 1–2 s:** mammal herds ([Torney et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29581404/)).
- **About one day:** tick questing, epibiont growth and detachment, allelochemical decay, egg and nest timers ([Tardy et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.220245); [Poyatos et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00227-025-04683-8); [Macías et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf048702l)).

A multi-rate scheduler should sub-step each movement class and advance the daily processes on an ecological tick. Rates quoted per day must be rescaled when simulated time is compressed. Scent persistence T_AS, for example, should be set relative to the time an agent takes to cross its range, not in absolute days ([Giuggioli et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002008)).

## Remaining gaps, stated plainly

Several parameters the simulator needs have **no sourced value** after this round. They must be exposed as calibration parameters, not presented as facts.

**Fields and chemistry.** No sourced release (exudation) rates exist for allelochemicals. There are no diffusion coefficients for allelochemicals in soil or water, and no "allelopathic radius" in metres. The only decay constants come from one benzoxazinoid family ([Macías et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1021/jf048702l)). Pheromone deposition and evaporation rates for specific ant species were not retrieved.

**Epibiosis and phoresy.** No primary measurement exists of the drag or energy cost of barnacles on turtles or whales. The −21% speed figure is a physics-based upper bound. Phoronts have no attachment or detachment probabilities. No quantified cost of epiphyte load to trees was found. Snäll et al.'s epiphyte colonisation and extinction estimates could not be read.

**Hematophagy and parasitism.** No movement-level model was found for tsetse host location, leech host-seeking or vampire-bat foraging flights. No tick engorgement multiplier or questing height was verified. No ABM couples flying-insect agents with herd steering, so the harassment-to-steering coupling proposed above is untested. Parasitoid perception radius and handling time were not verified.

**Aggression.** Literature values exist for DomWorld radii and StepDom (read via a summary), but not for force magnitudes. There are no cross-taxon allometries for fight energy cost or injury probability. No agent-based mobbing model with recruitment decay or a predator "move-on" threshold was found.

**Encounters.** The component exponents of Pawar et al. (velocity, detection distance) were not verified. Per-taxon detection distances and strike success rates were not collected. Per-flower handling time, nectar secretion rates and pollen transfer per visit were not verified. Published coefficients for gut-retention–mass allometry were not retrieved; the GRT ≈ 117·M^0.43 fit is the researchers' own.

**Movement.** Absolute speeds are missing for horses, primate troops, cockroaches, rodents and water striders. No 2020–2026 interaction-rule study was found for zebra, wildebeest, bison or elephant herds. Ginelli's rate functions are reconstructed, not quoted. The V-formation slot geometry is theoretical, not measured. Seabird and duck rafts on water, butterfly and dragonfly migration, mayfly swarm kinematics, and caddis or mayfly larval drift have no retrieved parameters.

**Vocabulary.** The live GloBI interaction-type API and OLS4 could not be reached. The mapping relies on source code and the RO release file, which are authoritative but may differ trivially from the deployed services. The full text of Kéfi et al. (2012) was not read, so its class names are inferred from the abstract and from Miele et al.'s implementation.

**Videos.** Few videos specific to non-trophic interactions were found. Videos of epibiosis or allelopathy models were not searched for. Supplementary movies are listed where the source papers confirm them.

## Conclusion

This round changes the problem from "twelve interactions to implement" to **five mechanisms to implement well**: consumer contact, contest, cargo, occupancy and field. The rest is parameter tables. That reduction is the main design result. It also explains why the formal vocabulary looks patchy: RO describes *who relates to whom*, while the simulator needs *what happens at the encounter*. The edge schema should therefore always carry a GloBI label, a nullable RO identifier and a behavioural mechanism, and never infer the last from the first two.

A second shift concerns where movement and interaction meet. In the earlier round, the coupling ran one way: steering was derived from the trophic matrix. The evidence here shows the coupling running back. Biting flies tighten herds and push them upwind. Seeds land where frugivores sleep. Gravid insects are drawn to polarising glass. A woodpecker's cavity limits a bluebird's breeding a year later. These feedbacks are what a boids-plus-network simulator can show and an equation-based food web cannot. The weakest links are physical constants, not behavioural rules: diffusion, release, drag and detachment. Calibrating those against the pattern table should come before any claim that the simulator is ecologically realistic.
